What peptides are

Peptides are short chains of amino acids, the same building blocks as protein. Your body makes thousands of them, and they act like messengers that tell cells what to do: release a hormone, signal fullness, start repair.

The ones people talk about in fitness fall into two very different groups, and knowing the difference is the most important thing on this page.

Two very different groups

1. FDA-approved prescription medications

The GLP-1 medications, like semaglutide and tirzepatide, are the best-known example. They went through clinical trials, they are approved for specific uses, and they are prescribed and monitored by a licensed clinician. For the right person, they can be life-changing.

2. Unapproved "research" peptides

Many peptides you see online, including several popular for recovery or muscle, are not FDA-approved for human use. Some are sold labeled "for research only, not for human consumption." That label is a warning, not a loophole: there is no guarantee of what is in the vial, how pure it is, or how it was made.

Red flags

  • Sold without a prescription or a consultation with a licensed clinician.
  • Labeled "research use only" or "not for human consumption."
  • No pharmacy name, no batch testing, or prices that seem too good to be true.
  • Big promises with no mention of side effects or who should not use it.
  • Dosing advice from a forum, an influencer or a seller instead of a clinician.

How to do it the right way

  • Start with labs and a real evaluation. A clinician should know your health history and current numbers first.
  • Prescription from a licensed clinician, filled by a licensed pharmacy.
  • Follow-up and monitoring, with labs repeated over time.
  • Keep the foundation. Peptides do not replace protein, training, sleep and a calorie target. They work best on top of them, which is exactly what coaching builds.
